The honest version

Building a wellness practice is harder than it looks from the outside.

The yoga teacher who is also managing their own bookings, writing their own newsletters, planning their own retreats, trying to figure out how to price fairly without undercharging, wondering why the Instagram post that took two hours only reached forty people. The breathwork facilitator who knows their work is good but cannot seem to make it financially viable. The somatic practitioner who is fully booked and still somehow not making enough to feel stable.

These are not personal failings. They are infrastructure problems. The tools were not built for this work. The business models were borrowed from industries that look nothing like wellness. And the support — real, experienced, values-aligned support — has been hard to find.
